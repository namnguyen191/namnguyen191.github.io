# Palette selection process

A step-by-step checklist for choosing and building the nam-ui colour palette. We work through it in order. Each step has:

- **Why**: what the step is for.
- **Understand**: concepts to learn before deciding anything.
- **Decide**: the choices this step produces.
- **Output**: what gets written down or changed in the repo.
- **Ready to move on**: checks that must all pass before starting the next step.
- **nam-ui today**: where the current palette stands on this step (from `TODO.md`).

One idea runs through the whole process: **we decide how the palette must behave before we pick any colours.** Hues come at step 5, after the rules they have to obey. That is the reverse of picking colours we like and checking them afterwards, which is how the current palette was made.

## Requirements

Decided in step 2 (complete). Each entry is recorded in the decisions log too.

- **Accessibility target:** WCAG 2.2 AA for every semantic colour pairing: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text (≥ 24px, or ≥ 18.66px bold) and for UI that identifies a control or its state. Body text aims for 7:1 (AAA). Decorative, logo and disabled colours are exempt, but disabled must still look less prominent than enabled. Covers colour only; other accessibility work belongs to the component packages. Also stated in the [README](./README.md#accessibility-standard).
- **Themes:** light and dark, designed together now (not dark added later). Every ramp must work in both themes, every semantic token gets a light and a dark value, and every pairing must meet the accessibility target in both. The system ships both themes and a simple way to select one; which theme an app shows (follow the OS, a user toggle, or always one theme) is the app's decision.
- **Output formats:** colours are published as `oklch()` in the main outputs (CSS, SCSS, JS); every browser released since 2023 and Dart Sass 1.79+ support it. A hex version of every colour is also published, generated by Style Dictionary from the same JSON, for consumers that can't read `oklch()` (React Native, email, Figma, tools that parse colours themselves). Its entry-point name is decided with the other JS export names before the first publish (`TODO.md` section 4).
- **Gamut:** every colour stays inside sRGB; no Display P3 for now. Everyone sees the same colour, contrast checks are exact, and every hex value is the nearest 8-bit match to its `oklch()` value. The `oklch()` value is the source (decided in step 8); rounding to hex moves lightness by at most 0.0014. P3 can be added later by raising chroma on individual tokens, without changing formats or names.
- **Brand constraints:** lilac is the brand: hue around 305° in OKLCH, with lilac's light, soft shade (today's `#d9bcf7`, about `oklch(0.84 0.09 307)`) as the signature colour. The brand is one hue, not one shade: the primary ramp runs from lilac tints to a dark, saturated purple, and each theme uses the steps that pass contrast on its background. Nothing uses the system yet, so every other colour, name and value can change.
- **Roles the palette serves:** `primary` (the lilac brand ramp), `neutral` (text, borders, surfaces) and four statuses: `error`, `warning`, `success`, `info` (hues decided in step 7). No secondary or tertiary brand colour. "Primary / secondary / tertiary" describe emphasis levels in components (for example filled, outlined and text-only buttons), made with the primary ramp and neutrals, not extra hues. The mazarine secondary role is dropped, and the unused palettes (turquoise, good-samaritan, dupain) are removed unless step 7 reuses one. An accent role can be added later if a real job for it appears; adding a role doesn't break anything, removing one does.
- **Out of scope:** a data-visualisation palette (its own process later if needed), illustration and marketing colours, Display P3, and high-contrast modes (Windows High Contrast / `forced-colors`, `prefers-contrast`), where the OS replaces colours and support is component work.
- **Existing values are not constraints:** apart from the lilac hue, every current colour (neutrals, statuses, other palettes) was picked without a process and gets rebuilt in steps 5–8.

## Lightness scale (step contract)

Decided in step 4. Every ramp (every hue, and the neutrals) puts each step at the same OKLCH lightness, so a step number means the same contrast in every hue. The dark theme's page background is neutral `950`.

| Step | OKLCH L | On white    | On dark page | Light theme job                                            | Dark theme job                                                                       |
| ---- | ------- | ----------- | ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 50   | 0.98    | 1.05–1.06   | 16.7–16.8    | Page and card tints, hover rows                            | Headings, highest-emphasis text                                                      |
| 100  | 0.95    | 1.14–1.17   | 15.2–15.5    | Page and card tints, hover rows; selected rows in `purple` | Headings, highest-emphasis text                                                      |
| 200  | 0.90    | 1.32–1.37   | 12.9–13.4    | Pressed rows, subtle dividers                              | Body text, link hover                                                                |
| 300  | 0.84    | 1.58–1.69   | 10.5–11.2    | Tints, badge backgrounds, decorative borders               | Secondary text, links, fills                                                         |
| 400  | 0.74    | 2.20–2.44   | 7.3–8.1      | Decorative only                                            | Icons, placeholder, focus ring                                                       |
| 500  | 0.64    | 3.17–3.61   | 4.92–5.60    | UI: borders, icons (≥ 3:1)                                 | Text and fills (≥ 4.5:1)                                                             |
| 600  | 0.54    | 4.73–5.49   | 3.23–3.75    | Text, solid fills with white text (≥ 4.5:1), focus ring    | UI: borders, icons (≥ 3:1)                                                           |
| 700  | 0.47    | 6.43–7.34   | 2.42–2.76    | Fill hover, link hover                                     | Decorative only                                                                      |
| 800  | 0.39    | 9.17–10.14  | 1.75–1.93    | Body text (≥ 7:1)                                          | Hover and pressed rows in cards, subtle dividers; selected rows in cards in `purple` |
| 900  | 0.30    | 13.25–14.09 | 1.26–1.34    | Headings, highest-emphasis text                            | Raised surfaces, hover rows; selected rows on the page in `purple`                   |
| 950  | 0.21    | 17.55–17.90 | (background) | Headings, highest-emphasis text                            | Page background                                                                      |

Contrast ranges were measured across six hues (purple, blue, green, red, yellow, neutral) at draft chroma values and are not maintained: they describe the scale, while contrast is enforced on the pairings declared in steps 5–7, which the generator checks (step 9). On the final palette purple falls outside every range; every threshold still holds.

How the targets were chosen:

- **Thresholds first.** On white, text (4.5:1) needs L ≤ about 0.55 and UI (3:1) L ≤ about 0.65. On the dark page (L 0.21), text needs L ≥ about 0.62 and UI L ≥ about 0.52. Between about 0.55 and 0.62 nothing is readable as text on both backgrounds, which is why each theme needs its own mapping.
- **Crossover pair.** `600` (L 0.54) is just inside text-on-white; `500` (L 0.64) is just inside text-on-dark and UI-on-white. Each is the other theme's UI step.
- **Ends.** `50` (L 0.98) is a near-white tint. `950` (L 0.21) is dark but not black: pure black makes light text appear to glow and leaves no room for darker surfaces below raised ones.
- **Spacing by job.** Steps near the ends are 0.03–0.06 apart, because surface layers should differ subtly. Middle steps are 0.07–0.10 apart, so a one-step hover change is always visible.
- **The dark column mirrors the light column:** `50` ↔ `950`, `200` ↔ `800`, `500` ↔ `600`.
- **Lilac lands exactly on `purple-300`.** Step `300` is at L 0.84 (lilac is 0.839). Since step 8, purple-300 isn't pinned: the purple rule (hue 306.9, 84% of maximum chroma) generates `oklch(0.84 0.0856 306.9)`, which rounds to exactly `#d9bcf7`, and the generator fails if it ever doesn't.

**Strength is distance from the background.** Step numbers mean the same lightness in both themes (`50` lightest, `950` darkest). In the light theme the background is near `50`, so higher numbers are stronger; in the dark theme the background is `950`, so lower numbers are stronger.

**Interaction states:** hover is one step from resting, pressed is two steps, and both move away from the background (darker in the light theme, lighter in the dark theme). In the middle of the ramp one step is at least 0.07 in L, so changes are always visible; near the ends steps are 0.03–0.06, so surface hovers stay subtle. Moving away from the background also means text on a fill only gains contrast as states change. That holds for text on solid fills (buttons). On surfaces (rows, cards) it's the reverse: a hover row or raised card moves the background toward the text, so the text loses contrast. See the pairing rule in [Neutral ramp](#neutral-ramp-step-6).

| Example                                 | Resting | Hover | Pressed |
| --------------------------------------- | ------- | ----- | ------- |
| Light theme primary button (white text) | 600     | 700   | 800     |
| Dark theme primary button (dark text)   | 300     | 200   | 100     |
| Light theme list row                    | page    | 50    | 100     |
| Dark theme list row                     | 950     | 900   | 800     |

Exceptions: **focus** is a separate ring at ≥ 3:1 against its surroundings (`600` light, `400` dark, chosen in step 9 so the ring passes on every surface it can sit on: page, card, hover and pressed rows), not a lightness change. **Selected** never relies on a background tint alone; it needs a checkmark, indicator or text that reaches 3:1. **Disabled** moves toward the background (low-contrast neutrals) and must look less prominent than enabled; exact tokens in step 10.

## Neutral ramp (step 6)

Decided in step 6. The neutral primitive is named `slate` (`palette.slate.50`–`950`; the `neutral` role points at it). Hue 257 (cool blue-grey), with chroma tapered so it's lowest at the ends and highest in the middle. Values below are drafts at the step 4 lightness targets; step 8 regenerates every ramp and may fine-tune chroma.

**Why slate.** At fixed lightness the tint changes contrast by less than 0.1 at every step (pure grey, lilac-tinted, slate and warm greys were all compared), so the tint is a look-and-feel choice. Slate keeps the UI cool and calm and leaves lilac as the only purple on screen: the brand reads as an accent rather than being present in every grey. Known costs: slate sits close to a conventional info blue; measured, a blue info surface at `100` can't be told apart from slate `100` by colour alone (ΔE_OK 0.017, handled in step 7), and at this chroma the dark page reads slightly navy, which was judged acceptable.

**Surfaces.** White is not a ramp step; it's a separate primitive used for light-theme cards and inputs.

| Surface                                                   | Light theme                         | Dark theme                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Page                                                      | `50`                                | `950`                                            |
| Card, raised surface, input                               | white (1.06:1 against the page)     | `900` (1.29:1 against the page)                  |
| Row on the page                                           | `50` → hover `100` → pressed `200`  | `950` → hover `900` → pressed `800`              |
| Row in a card                                             | white → hover `100` → pressed `200` | `900` → hover `800` → pressed: open (see below)  |
| Row selected (step 10; stays the same on hover and press) | `purple-100`                        | `purple-900` on the page, `purple-800` in a card |
| Card border, dividers                                     | `200` (decorative)                  | `800` (decorative)                               |

In the light theme, rows on white skip `50` for hover: at 1.06:1 it was too subtle to read as feedback. **Selected** rows keep a subtle tint plus an indicator (bar, checkmark) that reaches 3:1 against the row it sits in: `600` or darker in the light theme, `300` or `400` in the dark theme. The tint is the brand hue at the hover step (step 10): `200` / `800` would look like a pressed row. On the tints, the indicator is 4.31:1 (`slate-600` on `purple-100`) and 4.55 / 6.26 (`slate-400` on `purple-800` / `purple-900`). The tint itself can't carry selection: a 3:1 row background would be `500`, and body text on it would drop to 2.85:1.

**Text and borders.** Each step is the lightest (light theme) or darkest (dark theme) that passes on the **worst surface that text can sit on**, not just the page.

| Job               | Light | Worst case (light)    | Dark  | Worst case (dark)    | Threshold                                     |
| ----------------- | ----- | --------------------- | ----- | -------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Heading           | `950` | 13.1 on pressed `200` | `50`  | 9.1 on hover `800`   | 4.5                                           |
| Body              | `800` | 7.1 on pressed `200`  | `200` | 7.1 on hover `800`   | 4.5 (aim 7)                                   |
| Secondary, helper | `700` | 5.0 on pressed `200`  | `300` | 5.9 on hover `800`   | 4.5                                           |
| Placeholder       | `600` | 5.0 on input (white)  | `400` | 5.9 on input (`900`) | 4.5                                           |
| Disabled text     | `400` | 2.2 on page `50`      | `600` | 2.7 on card `900`    | exempt, must look less prominent than enabled |
| Input border      | `500` | 3.2 on page `50`      | `500` | 4.1 on card `900`    | 3.0                                           |

**Pairing rule.** Check a text or border step against every surface it can appear on, including hover, pressed and raised surfaces. The step 4 contrast columns were measured on white and on `950`, the two most extreme backgrounds; every surface between them costs contrast. Checking steps one at a time missed three failures in the first draft of this mapping (light secondary `600` on hover `100` = 4.36, dark secondary `500` on card `900` = 4.07, dark input border `600` on card `900` = 2.72).

## Status hues (step 7)

Decided in step 7. Primitive names follow the hue: `red`, `amber`, `green`, `cyan`.

| Status  | OKLCH hue              | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------- | ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Error   | 27                     | Plain red, between crimson (22, closest to success for deutans: ΔE_OK 0.032) and tomato (32, closest to warning: 0.117 vs 0.136).                                                                                                                                                 |
| Warning | 75 (amber, every step) | One hue for the whole ramp instead of yellow drifting to orange. Yellow can't be vivid at text lightness (max C 0.111 at L 0.54 vs 0.173 at L 0.84), so the ramp is amber throughout. Fills follow the lilac-button pattern: a light step (`300`) with dark text (`950`, 10.8:1). |
| Success | 158                    | Still clearly green, nudged toward teal: the deutan difference from error rises from 0.046 (at 150) to 0.063, while the gap from info stays 0.089 (it falls to 0.073 at 165).                                                                                                     |
| Info    | 212                    | Middle of the 205–220 window set by slate: info `100` is ΔE_OK 0.030 from slate `100` (0.024 at 220 is too close to the 0.02 threshold; 205 leans teal and nears success).                                                                                                        |

**Measured under colour vision deficiency** (simulated with Machado et al. 2009, draft chroma; step 8 sets final chroma):

| Pair                                   | Normal | Protan | Deutan    |
| -------------------------------------- | ------ | ------ | --------- |
| Error vs warning, text at `600`        | 0.137  | 0.077  | **0.006** |
| Error vs success, text at `600`        | 0.263  | 0.121  | 0.063     |
| Warning fill `300` vs error fill `600` | n/a    | n/a    | 0.295     |
| Info vs brand, surfaces at `100`       | 0.046  | n/a    | **0.003** |

**Re-measured in step 9** on the final palette (step 8 chroma), so the table above is draft only: error vs success text `600` is 0.154 / 0.062 / **0.019** (normal / protan / deutan), and 0.017–0.027 for deutans at every status step; error vs warning text `600` is 0.069 / 0.057 / 0.039; warning fill `300` vs error fill `600` is 0.302 for deutans; info vs brand surfaces `100` is 0.006 for deutans.

Because the step contract gives every hue the same lightness at each step, statuses at the same step differ only in hue, which is what red–green colour blindness loses (error and warning text are identical to a deutan). So:

- **Every status always has an icon and a text label.** WCAG 1.4.1 requires it, and here it's the only thing separating several pairs.
- **Use lightness where the job allows it.** Warning's light fill (`300`) against error's dark fill (`600`) is 0.295 apart even for a deutan. Status surfaces start at `100`; `50` is too faint to tell apart from neutral `50`.
- **Success must not drift toward teal.** Success and info are only 0.015 apart under (rare) tritanopia.

**Steps for each job.** The same steps for all four statuses, except warning's fill. Worst case is the lowest value across the four statuses and every surface the colour can sit on: its own banner plus every page, card and row surface (light: white, `50`, hover `100`, pressed `200`, selected `purple-100`; dark: `950`, `900`, `800`, selected `purple-900` / `purple-800`). Rows were added in step 10, which moved dark text and icons one step lighter (were `400` / `500`: red text on a hovered row was 3.95:1, red icon 2.71).

| Job                                  | Light theme                        | Worst case (light)          | Dark theme                         | Worst case (dark)       | Threshold |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------- |
| Text (inline message, banner, row)   | `700`                              | 4.87 (info, pressed row)    | `300`                              | 5.68 (error, hover row) | 4.5       |
| Icon                                 | `600`                              | 3.57 (success, pressed row) | `400`                              | 3.95 (error, hover row) | 3.0       |
| Input border (error state)           | `500`                              | 3.17 (success)              | `500`                              | 3.74 (error, on card)   | 3.0       |
| Solid fill: rest → hover → pressed   | `600` → `700` → `800`, white label | 4.81 (success, rest)        | `300` → `200` → `100`, `950` label | 10.72 (error, rest)     | 4.5       |
| Warning fill: rest → hover → pressed | `300` → `400` → `500`, `950` label | 5.22 (pressed)              | `300` → `200` → `100`, `950` label | 10.78 (rest)            | 4.5       |
| Banner background / border           | `100` / `200` (decorative)         | n/a                         | `900` / `800` (decorative)         | n/a                     | n/a       |

Notes:

- **Headroom on purpose.** Error alone would pass with text `600` and icon `500` (4.76 and 3.14 on its banner), but only because red's chroma adds contrast. Green and cyan lose contrast at the same lightness: success text `600` would be 4.21 and icon `500` 2.78, both failing. Choosing `700` / `600` lets one mapping serve all four statuses.
- **Tight spots for step 9:** success border `500` on white (3.17), success and info solid fills with white labels (4.81, 4.89). Final chroma from step 8 can move these by a few hundredths.
- **Dark banner `900`, not `950`:** a `950` banner has the same lightness as the page (1.02:1) and is separated by hue alone; `900` adds lightness (ΔE_OK 0.147 vs 0.089), which survives colour blindness better.
- **Dark-theme solid fills follow the lilac button** (light fill, dark label), so labels gain contrast in each state and destructive buttons stand out from the page (10.5:1) and from cards (8.1:1). A darker "tonal" destructive button (`900` → `800` → `700`, label `200`, all ≥ 5.4:1) is a candidate low-emphasis variant for step 10.
- **Light-theme warning fill** loses label contrast as it darkens (10.8 → 7.6 → 5.2), the same trade-off already accepted for the lilac primary button in step 5.

## Ramp generation (step 8)

Decided in step 8. Every colour is `oklch(L C H)` with L from the step contract, H from the ramp, and C from the ramp's rule. Generated by `src/scripts/palette_generator/generate_palette_script.ts`.

| Ramp                            | Hue              | Chroma rule                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `purple`                        | 306.9            | **Relative:** 0.84 × purple's own maximum chroma at each step                                        |
| `red`, `amber`, `green`, `cyan` | 27, 75, 158, 212 | **Equal:** 0.9 × the lowest maximum among the four status hues at each step, the same C for all four |
| `slate`                         | 257              | **Fixed:** the step 6 values                                                                         |

Maximum chroma is found by binary search (12 rounds from 0.4, precision below 0.0001), keeping L and H fixed and testing each guess in linear sRGB. The search only returns values that were tested inside sRGB, and the shares below 1 keep every colour off the gamut edge, so nothing is ever clamped.

Generated chroma:

| Step     | 50    | 100   | 200   | 300   | 400   | 500   | 600   | 700   | 800   | 900   | 950   |
| -------- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| purple   | .0100 | .0255 | .0521 | .0856 | .1454 | .2100 | .2332 | .2029 | .1684 | .1295 | .0906 |
| statuses | .0087 | .0223 | .0468 | .0792 | .1160 | .1003 | .0846 | .0737 | .0611 | .0470 | .0329 |
| slate    | .004  | .008  | .013  | .018  | .025  | .030  | .032  | .032  | .030  | .028  | .026  |

Notes:

- **Lilac is exact without a pin.** `purple-300` comes out as `#d9bcf7`. Purple's chroma at `300` must land between about 0.0855 and 0.0865 for that to hold, so the generator compares `purple-300` with `#d9bcf7` and fails on any difference (for example after a change to purple's share or to step `300`'s lightness).
- **Purple stands out.** At `500`–`900` purple has two to three times the statuses' chroma, so brand elements outrank status colours. At `50`–`300` there's little room for any hue, and purple and statuses end up about equal.
- **Statuses are held to the weakest hue.** Red is limited at `50`–`300`, cyan at `400`–`950`. Red could reach about 0.22 at `600` but gets 0.085.
- **Accepted trade-off: muted dark status steps.** At `500`–`700` error reads slightly brown (`red-600` `#9a5b54`) and warning tan (`amber-600` `#8b6731`); established systems use much more chroma (Tailwind v4 `red-600` is C 0.245). Kept for a consistent, recognisable look across the four statuses; every status carries an icon and a label, and every step 7 pairing still passes. Alternatives compared: relative chroma for statuses at every step (dark steps clearly red and green, but green and cyan tints at `200`–`300` turn neon and banners stop matching), a hybrid (equal for `50`–`300`, relative below), a capped relative rule, and moving warning toward yellow (dark steps turn olive) or orange (closer to error).

**Output.** The generator writes `tokens/primitive/palette.json` directly, one DTCG 2025.10 colour object per token:

```json
"300": { "$value": { "colorSpace": "oklch", "components": [0.84, 0.0856, 306.9], "hex": "#d9bcf7" } }
```

- **`oklch()` is the source value; hex is the fallback** for consumers that can't read `oklch()`. nam-ui is web-first, and every current browser reads `oklch()`.
- **The two can't be identical.** Hex has 256 levels per channel, so the hex is the nearest 8-bit colour, not an exact copy (lilac's green channel is 188.34 before rounding). Across all 66 colours rounding moves L by at most 0.0014 and C by at most 0.001; hue moves up to 9° only on near-greys (C ≤ 0.013), where hue is barely visible.
- **Precision:** L and H as designed, C rounded to 4 decimals. The hex is computed from the rounded value that's published.
- **Contrast checks (step 9) test both spellings** and pass only if both pass; rounding changes contrast by about 0.01.
- **Style Dictionary 5.7.1 reads the colour object** (it converts with `colorjs.io`). But the built-in `css` transform group outputs every colour as hex, so publishing `oklch()` in CSS needs a custom transform in `style-dictionary.config.ts`; done when the role and semantic tokens are reworked.
- **The generator checks before it writes:** every hex fallback within 0.002 of its lightness target, `purple-300` exactly `#d9bcf7`, and `white` exactly `#ffffff`. Any failure stops the script with the full list, and `palette.json` is left unchanged.
- **Also generated:** `palette.white` (`oklch(1 0 0)`, `#ffffff`), the separate primitive for light-theme cards and inputs from step 6. `palette.black` isn't generated; step 10 adds it if a token needs it.

## Semantic tokens (step 10)

Decided in step 10. Components use only these tokens. Each one aliases a role step (`{color.neutral.800}`), has an explicit light and dark value even when they're equal (each was chosen against its own worst-case surface), and carries its "Use for" text as `$description`, which Style Dictionary writes into the generated files as a comment.

**Naming rules**

1. **`category.meaning.state`, with `$root` for the resting state.** In DTCG a node is a token or a group, never both, so a group's own value goes in the reserved `$root` key (DTCG 2025.10). Style Dictionary 5.7.1 drops it from names: `fill.primary.$root` → `--nam-fill-primary`, `fill.primary.hover` → `--nam-fill-primary-hover`.
2. **Two kinds of background.** A `surface` is passive (page, card, row, banner); a `fill` is a control's background (button, input). The category says which foreground goes on it: `surface.error` takes `text.error`, `fill.error` takes `text.on-error`.
3. **Context goes in the name when the dark value depends on it.** Rows belong to their container (`surface.page.row-hover` is `slate-900` in dark, `surface.card.row-hover` is `slate-800`).
4. **One word, one meaning.** Primary / secondary / tertiary are button emphasis levels (step 2), so step 6's neutral "secondary text" is `text.subtle`.
5. **`subtle` means decorative:** no contrast requirement. Names without it have a threshold.

`{status}` stands for `error`, `success`, `info` and `warning`; steps are on that status's ramp. White is `color.white`, a role-tier sibling of the ramps (ramps stay numbers-only).

## Step 9: Validate the primitives

**Why:** Check that the generated values actually keep the contract before anything depends on them.

**Understand**

- [x] Why a lightness target doesn't guarantee contrast: WCAG measures relative luminance (Y), not OKLCH L. The two agree for greys and drift apart as chroma rises, in a direction set by hue: Y counts green light about 10× as much as blue, so purple and red come out darker than slate at the same L (more contrast on white, less on the dark page), and green and cyan lighter. The generator checks L, so only a contrast check can catch this.
- [x] Thresholds and headroom: WCAG pass/fail is strict (4.499:1 fails 4.5). Headroom is the distance above the threshold, and it gets used up by hex rounding (the two spellings differ by about 0.01–0.02), by later edits to chroma or surfaces, and by checkers that compute slightly differently. Either spelling can be what a user sees (`oklch()` in browsers, hex in native apps, email, Figma), so a pairing's contrast is the lower of the two.
- [x] Neighbouring steps: "visibly different" is measured with ΔE_OK, not the contrast ratio, which only sees luminance (`red-600` vs `green-600` is 1.09:1 but ΔE_OK 0.154). About 0.02 is the just-noticeable difference (JND) side by side under good conditions; a state change replaces one colour with another over time on imperfect screens, so it needs several times that. Measure a state change from the colour it starts on.
- [x] Equal visual weight across hues: OKLCH L, WCAG Y and the eye can each give a different answer. At the same L, `purple-600` has a lower Y than `slate-600` (its light is mostly red and blue, which Y counts little), while the Helmholtz–Kohlrausch effect (vivid colours look brighter than a grey of the same luminance, not modelled by OKLab) makes it look at least as light. For accessibility the formula (WCAG Y) overrules the eye; for "looks consistent" the eye overrules the formula, so this check is done by looking (squint, one column at a time, mostly `500`–`800`). L is fixed by the contract, so the only levers are C and H, and either one changes Y, so contrast has to be checked again after any adjustment.
- [x] Colour vision deficiency simulation across the whole palette: a matrix (Machado et al. 2009) maps each colour to how it looks without one cone type, then ΔE_OK is measured as usual. Any colour difference splits into OKLab's three axes: L (light–dark), a (green–red), b (blue–yellow). Protanopia and deuteranopia mostly flatten a, tritanopia flattens b, so a pair is at risk from the type that flattens the axis it differs along (`red-600` vs `green-600` differ almost only in a and drop to 0.019 for deutans; `amber-600` vs `purple-600` differ mostly in b and drop to 0.069 for tritans). Same-step pairs have no L difference by contract, so lightness can't rescue them; pairs at different steps keep their L difference under every type.
- [x] Checking on real screens: every number assumes an ideal sRGB screen. Real screens differ in gamut and colour management (vivid purples can turn neon on unmanaged wide-gamut screens or dull on cheap ones), black level (near-black steps run together), glare and night modes (warmer screens shift blue, purple and cyan). Glare adds the same light to every colour, which barely moves light colours but lifts dark ones a lot, so it hurts the dark theme most. A failure on one screen is usually fixed in the component (a border, a different surface step), not in the primitives.

**Decide**

- [x] Pass/fail policy (replaces the earlier warn-below-+0.2 rule): no warnings and no headroom margin. A contrast pairing fails below its WCAG threshold (4.5:1 text, 3:1 UI) in either spelling; a neighbouring pair fails below ΔE_OK 0.02 (the JND). A pairing can carry a written exception (`exception: 'reason'`), which works like `@ts-expect-error`: an excepted pairing that fails passes and is printed with its reason and value; an excepted pairing that passes is itself a failure ("exception no longer needed"), so exceptions can't go stale. Exceptions belong to a pairing, not a colour.

- [x] Colour vision deficiency: error and success are at or below the just-noticeable difference for deutans at every status step (0.017–0.027) on the final palette. Accepted: every status always shows an icon and a text label (step 7 rule), so colour is a supporting cue, not the signal. Status chroma stays as decided in step 8, and the four statuses keep one shared step mapping.

- [x] What the script checks: contrast in both spellings (see the pass/fail policy above) and ΔE_OK between neighbouring steps. Not scripted: colour vision deficiency (no pass/fail rule after accepting error vs success; checked in the browser with DevTools vision-deficiency emulation once components exist), glare (a rough model, not a rule) and visual weight (needs eyes).
- [x] Contrast scope: the pairings declared in steps 5–7 (about 130: each job against every surface it can sit on), not a table of every step against fixed backgrounds. Pairings are what components actually use; a new job means adding its pairings to the list (the step 6 pairing rule). The list is a temporary copy of the step 5–7 tables: in step 10 it's generated from the semantic tokens instead.
- [x] Where it runs: inside `generate_palette_script.ts`, next to the existing checks; colour formulas (relative luminance, contrast ratio, ΔE_OK) go in `color-helpers.ts`. Results print to the terminal; any failure stops the script before `palette.json` is written, like the existing checks.

**Found so far**

- Step 8 gave purple more than twice the statuses' chroma at `500`–`900`, so purple is now outside every step 4 contrast range measured at draft chroma (for example `purple-500` on the dark page is 4.76, documented as 4.92–5.60). Every step 4 _threshold_ still passes; the documented _ranges_ are stale. The ranges stay as draft guidance; the generator checks the declared pairings instead (see Decide).
- Neighbouring steps: the smallest gap is `slate-50` → `100` (ΔE_OK 0.030, 1.5× the JND); every middle-ramp pair is ≥ 0.070. Light rows in a white card hover to `100` (0.051 from white) because `50` would be 0.020, at the JND. Dark rows in a card hover `900` → `800` (0.090).
- Light card vs page: white on `slate-50` is ΔE_OK 0.020, at the JND, so the card's fill alone doesn't show its edge. The `slate-200` card border from step 6 (0.101 from white) or a shadow (an elevation token, later) has to. Dark cards are separated by lightness instead (`900` on `950`, 0.090), because shadows barely show on a near-black page.

Red and green differ almost entirely along OKLab's red–green axis, which deuteranopia removes, and the step contract gives them the same lightness, so nothing else separates them. Error vs warning moved the other way (0.006 → 0.039). Lightness-separated pairs hold (warning fill vs error fill 0.30).

## Step 10: Map role and semantic tokens

**Why:** Connect the primitives to the jobs components actually need done, so every pairing a component uses has a known, checked contrast.

**Understand**

- [x] Role tier: each role (primary, neutral, error…) points at one ramp. Only ramps with a job exist. The tier is aliases only: one line per role (`primary → purple`, `neutral → slate`, `error → red`, `warning → amber`, `success → green`, `info → cyan`), spelled out for all 11 steps. It holds no job decisions (no `default`) and no theme logic: a step number is a lightness in every tier and theme, and the dark theme isn't a flip of the light one (the primary button is `300` with a `950` label in both; a flipped dark input border would be `600`, 2.72:1 on a card). The semantic tier picks the step per job and per theme. A hue swap is a one-line edit but not a safe one: Y differs by hue at the same L, and the shared status chroma means changing one status hue moves all four (info at 250 instead of 212: status chroma at `400`–`950` rises about 20%, now limited by amber; `info-100` vs `slate-100` drops from 0.018 to 0.014). Every ramp in `palette.json` already has a role; step 8 removed turquoise, good-samaritan and dupain.
- [x] Semantic categories: text, icon, border, surface, plus "on-X" tokens for content placed on solid fills. Categories follow contrast rules: text 4.5:1, icons 3:1, borders 3:1 when they identify a control (`border.input`) and exempt when decorative (card border), surfaces are the background side of every pairing. A foreground token is valid only on the surfaces it was chosen for, so pick it by asking what it sits on: neutral or subtle status surfaces take `text.error` (`red-700` on `red-100` 6.06:1), the solid fill takes `text.on-error` (`red-700` there would be 1.35:1; white is 5.25:1). On-X tokens are per role because the labels differ (light: white on error, `950` on warning). Each status has a subtle surface (banner) and a solid one (button, badge). Light and dark values stay explicit even when equal, because each was chosen against its own worst-case surface.
- [x] Interaction states: hover, pressed, selected, focus, disabled. Each needs a visible change, and disabled must look less prominent than enabled. Rules from step 4 (hover one step, pressed two, both away from the background; selected needs an indicator ≥ 3:1; disabled moves toward the background) and step 6 (row states, selected-row indicator). What's still open is listed under Decide.
- [x] Focus indicators: 2.4.7 Focus Visible (AA) requires a visible indicator; 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast (AA) requires 3:1 against adjacent colours; 2.4.13 Focus Appearance (AAA) asks for roughly a 2px ring that changes by 3:1 between unfocused and focused. 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (AA) is about layout (nothing covering the focused element), not colour. The ring from step 9 (`purple-600` light, `purple-400` dark, ≥ 3:1 on every surface) meets the AA criteria.

**Decide**

- [x] Scope: map only the jobs already decided in steps 4–9 (each has a chosen step in both themes and a pairing). Every other token, including the rest of `TODO.md` section 3 (`link-visited`, `inverse`, `overlay`/`scrim`…), is added when a component needs it, with its pairings.
- [x] A mapping table: semantic token → role → step, light and dark. See [Semantic tokens](#semantic-tokens-step-10), with the naming rules, a `fill` category for control backgrounds, `text.subtle` (was "secondary text"), neutral icons (`icon.default` `700` / `300`, `icon.subtle` `600` / `400`; not pure black or white, which glows on the dark page and clashes with slate text), and dark link hover `purple-200` (step 4's `400` moved toward the background and was 3.94:1 on a hovered card row).
- [x] Give the unused primitives (turquoise, good-samaritan, dupain) a role, or remove them. Removed in step 8.
- [x] Selected-row tint: the brand hue at the hover step, the same on hover and press: `purple-100` light, `purple-900` on the dark page and `purple-800` in a dark card. A neutral tint at `200` / `800` would look pressed; `purple-200` (more visible) drops links to 4.21:1. The indicator (≥ 3:1) carries selection, as decided in step 6.
- [x] Dark status text and icons move one step lighter: text `300`, icon `400` (were `400` / `500`), so they pass on hovered and selected rows. Cost: lower chroma (0.079 at `300` vs 0.116 at `400`), so dark statuses look paler.
- [x] Component rule: a row that can be pressed contains no links or other interactive elements (no interactive element inside another). So links are checked on hover and selected rows but not pressed ones (light `purple-600` on pressed `200` is 4.25:1).
- [x] Disabled: one neutral treatment for every role, so a disabled control loses its hue (a disabled red button still says "danger"; grey says "not available"). Fill `slate-100` / `slate-800`, label on it `slate-400` / `slate-600`, border `slate-200` / `slate-700`, text `slate-400` / `slate-600` (light / dark). Opacity (Material's approach) was rejected: the result depends on what's behind it, so it can't be checked.
- [x] Secondary button (replaces step 5's neutral outline): no fill at rest, a decorative `purple-300` / `purple-700` border (about 1.7–2.3:1, the same softness in both themes), label `purple-700` / `purple-300`, hover fill `purple-100` / `purple-900`, pressed `purple-200` / `purple-800` (light / dark). The filled lilac primary still leads, because a fill outweighs an outline. Hover starts at `100`: `purple-50` is ΔE 0.008 from the page, invisible, as step 6 found for rows. An outline button's fill moves toward its label as states progress (the reverse of a filled button), so the light label is `purple-700` in every state (7.62 / 6.53 / 5.57:1); `purple-600` would be 4.19 when pressed. The border stays the same in every state.
- [x] Pressed rows inside dark cards: the background stays at the hover step (`800`, step 9) and no colour token is added. Any extra effect (a glow, an inset outline) isn't a palette colour, so it's designed with the row component. The semantic token records this in its `$description`.

**Found so far**

- Step 7's status pairings and step 5's links were checked only on the page, card and the status's own banner, so the step 9 checker never tested them on rows. Status text sits in rows all the time (a "Failed" cell). On a hovered dark row (`slate-800`) status text `400` was 3.95–4.36:1 and the red icon (`500`) 2.71. The checker now tests links and statuses on every row surface (200 pairs, all pass); reverting the dark status steps fails 11 pairs, as expected. Lesson: a token's list of surfaces is a decision in its own right.
- A disabled-state check (disabled contrast must stay below enabled contrast) was written and then removed the same day. On coloured controls it measured the wrong thing: hue and label colour, not fill contrast, separate a control from its grey disabled state (tonal buttons at `purple-50` / `purple-100` light and `red-900` dark: it flagged the clearly enabled ones and passed the one whose fill is closest to disabled). On neutral controls, the only place it applied, the margins are wide and the four values are fixed, so it would only guard against an unlikely edit.

**Ready to move on**

- [ ] Every semantic text/background, icon/background and border/background pairing passes.
- [x] Disabled states are less prominent than enabled ones. Measured, not scripted: input border `500` vs disabled `200` / `700` (3.36 vs 1.34 on white, 4.05 vs 1.99 on a dark card); placeholder vs disabled text (5.03 vs 2.29 light, 5.95 vs 2.69 dark); disabled fill vs the softest enabled fill, the lilac primary (1.15 vs 1.67 on white, 1.42 vs 8.12 on a dark card). Coloured controls also differ in hue. Re-check by eye in step 12.

**nam-ui today:** `border.secondary` points at the primary colour; disabled borders and labels are stronger than enabled ones; no `on-error` / `on-success` / `on-warning` / `on-info` tokens.

## Step 11: Design the dark theme

**Why:** Dark mode is a requirement (step 2) and ships with the light theme. It reuses the same ramps with a different semantic mapping, so this step turns the dark-theme usage rules from step 4 into a full mapping.

**Understand**

- [ ] Dark mode is not colour inversion.
- [ ] Surfaces: avoid pure black; raised surfaces are lighter.
- [ ] Saturated colours look harsh on dark backgrounds, so text and fills usually move to lighter, less saturated steps.
- [ ] Contrast must be checked again: a step that passes on white may fail on a dark surface.
- [ ] Raised surfaces: dark cards (`900`) cost about 25% contrast compared with the page (`950`). Check every pairing on cards and on hover rows inside cards, not just on the page (see the open items in [Neutral ramp](#neutral-ramp-step-6)).

**Decide**

- [ ] Dark-theme mapping table (semantic token → step).

**Ready to move on**

- [ ] Every semantic pairing also passes in the dark theme.

## Glossary

Each term is described as it's used in this process.

**Colour models**

- **Hex / RGB**: how screens store colour: the amount of red, green and blue light, 0–255 each (`#d9bcf7` = 217, 188, 247). Values are gamma-encoded, so `#80` is not half the light of `#ff`.
- **HSL**: hue, saturation, lightness. A rearrangement of RGB. Its lightness is `(max channel + min channel) / 2`, which ignores which channels are lit, so the same L looks very different across hues (pure yellow and pure blue are both 50%). Not used for building ramps.
- **OKLCH**: L (lightness, 0–1, built so equal steps look equal), C (chroma: vividness, 0 = grey, no fixed maximum, roughly 0.37 at most), H (hue angle, 0–360°). Used for building ramps. CSS: `oklch(0.55 0.15 300)`.
- **Hue landmarks in OKLCH**: red ~25, orange ~60, yellow ~100, green ~145, cyan ~195, blue ~260, purple ~305, pink ~350.
- **Reading OKLCH (rough guide)**: L above 0.9 is a tint, 0.7–0.85 light, 0.5–0.65 medium, 0.3–0.45 dark, below 0.25 near-black. C below 0.03 is near-grey, 0.05–0.1 muted, 0.1–0.2 vivid, above 0.2 very vivid (only possible for some hues and lightnesses).

**Lightness and luminance**

- **Relative luminance (Y)**: the physical amount of light, 0 (black) to 1 (white), computed from linearized RGB: `Y = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B`. Green counts about 10× as much as blue because of how the eye's cones respond. WCAG contrast is based on Y.
- **Linearize**: convert a gamma-encoded channel back to linear light before doing maths on it. Contrast tools do this automatically.
- **Perceived lightness (CIE L\*, OKLCH L)**: how light a colour looks. Not linear in Y: about 18% luminance looks like middle grey (L\*50).

**Contrast and accessibility**

- **WCAG contrast ratio**: `(Y lighter + 0.05) / (Y darker + 0.05)`, from 1:1 to 21:1. The 0.05 accounts for screen glare. No rounding up: 4.49:1 fails (`#777777` on white is 4.48, `#767676` is 4.54).
- **4.5:1**: normal text at AA (WCAG 1.4.3).
- **3:1**: large text at AA (≥ 24px, or ≥ 18.66px bold), and non-text UI (WCAG 1.4.11): the visual information needed to identify a control or its state, such as an input border when the field has no fill, meaningful icons, focus indicators.
- **7:1**: normal text at AAA (WCAG 1.4.6).
- **Exempt from contrast rules**: disabled controls, logos, decoration, incidental text. Exempt doesn't mean unconstrained: disabled still has to look less prominent than enabled.
- **Below-threshold colours**: still useful for decoration, dividers already separated by spacing, and tints, as long as something else carries the meaning.
- **APCA**: the contrast method proposed for WCAG 3 (draft). It scores dark-on-light and light-on-dark differently, and fixes WCAG 2 overrating dark colours. We use it as a second opinion; WCAG 2.2 AA is the target.
- **WCAG 2.2 AA**: the level referenced by law (for example Ontario's AODA requires WCAG 2.0 AA; EU accessibility rules reference 2.1 AA).

**Colour vision deficiency (CVD)**

- **Protan / deutan**: missing or weak red / green cones; red–green confusion; about 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women of Northern European ancestry. Protans also see reds as darker.
- **Tritan**: blue–yellow confusion; rare.
- **Lightness survives CVD better than hue**: status colours should differ in lightness or carry an icon or text.
- **WCAG 1.4.1, use of colour**: colour must never be the only signal (for example, links in body text need an underline or another non-colour cue).

**Gamut**

- **Gamut**: the range of colours a screen or format can show.
- **sRGB**: the standard web gamut; every hex value is inside it.
- **Display P3**: about 25% larger; most Apple devices and many modern phones.
- **Clipping / gamut mapping**: OKLCH can describe colours outside sRGB; they are pulled back (chroma reduced) to the nearest showable colour.
- **Clamping**: forcing each RGB channel into 0–100% (negative becomes 0, above 100% becomes 100%) without regard to L, C or H. It always produces a showable colour, but L, C and H can all move, uncontrolled. Happens silently when an out-of-gamut colour is converted to hex or shown on an sRGB screen.
- **Chroma reduction**: the gamut-mapping method we use: keep L and H fixed and lower C until all three channels are within 0–100%. Lower chroma moves the channels toward equal (grey), so it always ends inside the gamut.
- **Maximum chroma (gamut boundary)**: the highest C a given L and H can have inside sRGB. Differs by hue and lightness; it sets the ceiling for each step of each ramp.
- **Equal chroma (strategy)**: every hue gets the same C at a step, so they look about equally vivid (same L and same C look roughly equal in OKLCH). The weakest hue at that step sets the ceiling for all.
- **Relative chroma (strategy)**: every hue gets the same percentage of its own maximum. Each ramp is as vivid as its hue allows, so hues with more room (purple, red) look much more vivid than hues with less (cyan).
- **Peak chroma differs by hue**: yellow is most vivid when very light (around L 0.9), blue when fairly dark (around L 0.45). A dark yellow can't be vivid, so it becomes olive or brown.

**Neutrals and surfaces**

- **Tinted neutral**: a grey with a very small chroma (about 0.005–0.03) toward a hue, so it still reads as grey but has a temperature. At fixed lightness it barely changes contrast.
- **Simultaneous contrast**: the eye shifts a colour away from what's next to it; a pure grey beside a saturated purple can look faintly green or cold. Tinted neutrals reduce this.
- **Surface layering**: page, card, hover and stripe colours differ by about 1.05–1.2:1. They aren't required to reach 3:1 because they don't carry required information; content, borders and selection indicators do.
- **Worst-case surface (pairing rule)**: choose a text or border step by checking it against the darkest (light theme) or lightest (dark theme) surface it can sit on, including hover, pressed and raised surfaces.
- **ΔE_OK (colour difference)**: the straight-line distance between two colours in OKLab. About 0.02 is the smallest difference most people notice (the threshold CSS Color 4 uses for gamut mapping). Two surfaces meant to look different should be well above it.

**Status colours**

- **Gamut cusp**: the lightness at which a hue reaches its highest possible chroma. Yellow's cusp is very light (around L 0.85–0.9), red's and blue's are mid to dark, which is why dark yellows look olive or brown.
- **Hue drift**: rotating a ramp's hue across its steps (for example yellow at the light end, orange at the dark end) so every step looks like the intended colour. nam-ui's ramps use one hue per ramp.
- **CVD simulation**: converting a colour to how it appears with protanopia, deuteranopia or tritanopia (we use the Machado et al. 2009 model, applied in linear RGB), then measuring ΔE_OK between the simulated colours.
